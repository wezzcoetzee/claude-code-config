# Reports

A report is read to understand something quickly. Monochrome keeps the eye on the content, and visuals carry what prose would bury.

## Palette

- True black `#000` background and white primary text wherever you control the page.
- Grays only for gridlines, axes, and rules.
- Color is a signal. Spend it on the one thing each visual is about: the series that matters, the number that moved, the step that failed. Use at most one accent plus status colors (red for bad, green for good).

## Visuals

Reach for a visual first and prose second. Each one answers a single question the reader has.

- Numbers over time or across categories: a chart.
- A flow, architecture, or sequence: a diagram.
- Options compared on several dimensions: a table.
- A headline number: a large figure with its delta.

Every chart gets a title that states the finding ("p95 latency halved after the cache"), labelled axes, and direct labels in place of a legend where they fit.

## Layout

- Information-dense: content over chrome. Real headings, tables, and diagrams carry the structure, with no decorative cards, pills, badges, or gradients.
- Each section opens on its heading and its conclusion, with no subtitle line above it.
- One idea per section. A section that is three sentences of throat-clearing gets cut.
